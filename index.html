01 / UNDERSTAND

What actually is AI?

Think of AI as software that learns patterns or uses rules to produce answers, predictions or decisions. It is not automatically intelligent in the way humans are.

Some AI recommends songs or detects spam. Generative AI creates text, images or code by learning statistical relationships from training examples. A chatbot predicts plausible next pieces of text; sounding confident does not mean it is correct.

02 / THE TRADE-OFF

Powerful tool.
Real consequences.

↗THE UPSIDE

What AI makes possible

  • Learning: explaining difficult ideas in different ways and offering practice questions.
  • Accessibility: captions, translation and assistive communication.
  • Work and research: spotting patterns in large datasets and speeding up repetitive tasks.

My view: the best use is supporting human thinking, not replacing it.

!THE DOWNSIDE

What we cannot ignore

  • False confidence: invented facts can sound convincing.
  • Bias and privacy: systems can reflect unfair data or expose sensitive information.
  • Manipulation: deepfakes make impersonation and scams more convincing.

My view: speed is not worth losing accuracy or accountability.

When seeing isn't believing.

VOICEFACETRUST?

A meeting full of people who weren't there.

In Hong Kong, an employee of engineering company Arup joined a video call featuring realistic fake colleagues, including a fake chief financial officer. The employee transferred around HK$200 million to scammers. Arup later confirmed it was the victim.

The lesson: a familiar face or voice is no longer enough proof. Verify unexpected payment requests through a separate, trusted channel.

04 / WHAT COMES NEXT

The future has
a power bill.

AI runs on physical infrastructure: chips, servers, cooling equipment and electricity.

The International Energy Agency estimates worldwide data-centre electricity use at about 485 TWh in 2025 and projects roughly 950 TWh by 2030. This is a forecast, not a certainty, and includes non-AI data-centre activity.

Rapid growth could put pressure on local electricity grids if infrastructure cannot keep up. Blackouts are a risk to manage, not an inevitable outcome. Concentration in major cloud providers also means outages can disrupt many services at once.

MY PREDICTION

AI will become more useful and more embedded in daily life. The deciding factor won't just be how capable it gets, but whether humans keep oversight, secure critical systems and invest in reliable energy.

How this website was made

I organized this website as a short visual story rather than a list of answers. It begins with a simple explanation of AI, moves into its benefits and risks, then examines a real deepfake scam and the electricity demands of data centres. I chose a dark background with bright green and purple accents to make the design feel technological while keeping the text readable.

I built the page with semantic HTML, including sections, headings, navigation and links. CSS handles the layout, typography, visual diagrams and responsive adjustments for smaller screens. A small JavaScript quiz gives visitors a practical way to think about verification. The graphics are made with CSS rather than external images, so the website loads without image dependencies.
